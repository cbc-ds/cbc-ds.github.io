According to Dijkstra, "if debugging is the process of removing bugs, programming must be the process of putting them there". In a notoriously challenging area as Distributed Systems, where bugs lead to serious failures, avoiding the introduction of bugs during system development is critical. Recent large-scale crashes caused by faulty system updates, as well as bugs in Decentralised Applications (DApps, typically running on Blockchains) that have enabled the diversion of vast amounts of funds from organisations relying on smart contracts, clearly demonstrate the need for solutions that produce safe, sound, and resilient systems as automatically as possible. Formal methods, a direct by-product of Logic in Computer Science, have been providing foundations, approaches, and tools to support the ambitious goal of constructing correct-by-construction distributed systems and DApps.

The goal of the workshop is to gather researchers and practitioners working in these foundational approaches, methods, and tools, leveraging, on one hand, the results of the TaRDIS project concerning swarm-like systems and, on the other hand, auto-active reasoning principles and platforms applied to decentralised distributed systems and applications.

The workshop will be an opportunity to present ongoing and recent research, possibly published or presented elsewhere, and to discuss in more depth the challenges and results, compare approaches and tools, and engage with experts in the field.
